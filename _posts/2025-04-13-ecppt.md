## Introduction

The eCPPTv3 is a practical certification focused on Active Directory and realistic attack techniques in enterprise environments. The exam takes place in a controlled environment and lasts 24 hours, during which you must complete a series of tasks and answer specific questions, without the need to write a final report.

---

## Why did I choose eCPPT?


When I decided to take the eCPPTv3, I knew it was a more advanced certification than the eJPT, with a significant focus on Active Directory and more realistic attack scenarios. I had chosen it hoping for a concrete upgrade compared to v2... but the reality was different from what I expected.

In this post I want to tell you about my experience and give you some useful advice on how to prepare

---

## Certification topics


**Information Gathering & Reconnaissance (10%)**
- Perform Host Discovery and Port Scanning on Target Networks
- Enumerate Information From Services Running on Open Ports


**Initial Access (15%)**
- Perform Username Enumeration to Identify Valid User Accounts on Target Systems
- Perform Password Spraying Attacks to Identify Valid Credentials for Initial Access
- Perform Brute-Force Attacks on Remote Access Services for Initial Access


**Web Application Penetration Testing (15%)**

- Perform Web Application Enumeration to Identify Potential Vulnerabilities & Misconfigurations
- Identify and Exploit Common Web Application Vulnerabilities For Initial Access (SQLi, XSS, Command Injection, etc)
- Perform Brute-Force Attacks Against Login Forms
- Exploit Vulnerable and Outdated Web Application Components
- Exfiltrate Data and Credentials From Compromised Web Applications and Databases


**Exploitation & Post-Exploitation (25%)**


- Identify and Exploit Vulnerabilities or Misconfigurations in Services
- Identify and Exploit Privilege Escalation Vulnerabilities
- Dump and Crack Password Hashes
- Identify Locally Stored Unsecured Credentials



**Exploit Development (5%)**
- Develop/Modify Exploit Code For Initial Access and Post-Exploitation
- Identify and Exploit Memory Corruption Vulnerabilities (Stack Overflow, Buffer Overflow)


**Active Directory Penetration Testing (30%)**


- Perform Active Directory Enumeration
- Identify Domain Accounts With Weak or Empty Passwords
- Perform AS-REP Roasting to Steal Kerberos Tickets for Authentication
- Perform Active Directory Lateral Movement Techniques (Pass-the-Hash, Pass-the-Ticket)
- Obtain Domain Admin Privileges/Access

## My experience

The exam, as you may know, is practical and lasts 24 hours. You no longer write a report, but you have to answer a series of technical questions based on what you can get from the target systems.

I managed to pass on the first try, but it wasn't easy. I already had a good technical base, but I had to make a leap in level to tackle Active Directory seriously.

I didn't take advantage of the official INE preparation due to the high cost. I integrated with external materials, advanced labs and a lot of practical practice.


---

## Practical advice for those who want to tackle eCPPT


**Study AD from HTB/THM Modules**

- If you already have experience with platforms like Hack The Box or TryHackMe, make the most of it. Practice on real environments is essential to understand attack and defense techniques.

**Practice enumeration on Windows machines**

- Tools come crackmapexec, kerbrute, bloodhound-python, smbclient, rpcclient



**Take structured notes**
- Organize your folder structure when running commands or saving scans.
I prefer this structure:
```bash
/enumeration
/exploitation
/privilege_escalation
/loot
/active_directory
```



**Manage your time well**

- 24 hours seems like a lot, but they fly by. Don't waste time trying in vain.

**Stay calm and focused**

- During the exam, you may encounter obstacles or unexpected situations. Keep calm, analyze the problem carefully and look for alternative solutions.

## What did eCPPT really give me?

The eCPPTv3 was definitely more challenging than the eJPT, but also much more educational. It pushed me to get out of the comfort zone of classic pentesting on single machines and to face more realistic scenarios, similar to those found in a corporate environment.

Here's what it really left me with:

- Concrete approach to Active Directory environments
Before the eCPPTv3 I only knew the theory, but with the exam I had to learn how to manage targeted attacks in complex infrastructures, with multiple hosts, exposed services and real authentication mechanisms.

- Ability to connect information
In structured environments, it's not enough to find a vulnerability: you have to reconstruct the chain that leads you to the objective. The certification helped me improve precisely this type of reasoning.

- Organization and strategy
Working in a large context and with little time forces you to be organized. I started to better manage my notes, commands, collected data — skills that I still use today in real projects.

- Awareness of my gaps
The eCPPTv3 made me understand what I still had to work on, especially on the AD side. Without this experience, perhaps I would have remained stuck on a more superficial level.

Even without the final report, the exam forces you to think like a consultant: you have to know what to look for, where to look for it and, above all, understand why certain information is important.
